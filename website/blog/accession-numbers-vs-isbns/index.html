Accession Numbers, ISBNs and Barcodes: A Librarian's Primer

Published November 15, 2026 · 4 min read · By Pusthakam Project

One of the most frequent points of confusion for new librarians or school administrators setting up a library catalogue is the relationship between three distinct identifiers: the ISBN, the Accession Number, and the Barcode.

1. The ISBN: Identifying the Work or Edition

The International Standard Book Number (ISBN) is an external identifier assigned by publishers to a specific edition or commercial format of a book. If your library purchases four copies of the paperback edition of To Kill a Mockingbird, all four copies will have the exact same 13-digit ISBN printed on the back cover.

Consequently, an ISBN cannot tell you which specific physical book was borrowed by student Alice, which copy has water damage, or which copy is missing from shelf 3B.

2. The Accession Number: Identifying the Physical Copy

The accession number is the internal, sequential identifier your library assigns to each physical object entering the collection. It is the core unit of the historic accession register.

In Pusthakam, bibliographic records (titles, authors, publication year, subjects) are linked one-to-many to physical copy records. Each copy has:

  • Its own unique accession number (e.g., ACC-0042, ACC-0043).
  • Its specific acquisition date and source (purchase, donation, government grant).
  • Its individual condition (available, damaged, in repair, withdrawn).
  • Its assigned physical location and shelf label.

3. The Barcode: Physical Encoding for the Scanner

A barcode is simply a machine-readable optical representation of an identifier. In most modern libraries, the barcode encodes the accession number or a dedicated item number using the Code 128 symbology.

Pusthakam automatically generates standard Code 128 vector barcode SVGs for every accession number. When printed on adhesive label sheets and affixed to the book cover or flyleaf, a standard USB keyboard-wedge barcode scanner can read the code instantly in a fraction of a second.

Best Practice Recommendation

Never use the publisher's printed EAN-13 ISBN as your circulation barcode if you have multiple copies of any title. Always assign a distinct accession number to each physical copy and scan the accession barcode during desk circulation.
